# Phase 3: Review

Goal: decide what every analyzer and KiCad result means for this board, then look for what no rule can see. You have {phase3_calls} tool calls and {images} images at most for the whole review. Spend them on judgement, not browsing.

The server will not let you leave this phase until both of these are true:
- every rule group from `list_candidates` is resolved: its results are recorded in findings or dismissed with a reason (one finding can cover a group);
- you have taken at least {region_views} close-up region view(s) with `view_board` (an `around` target or a `region`, not the whole board);
- the layout has been judged (see below): at least {layout_findings} finding(s) from the layout groups or anchored open-review opinions, or a specific reason for each layout group with none.
A refused `advance_phase` costs one call. If the calls run out the review moves on and everything unreviewed is listed as not reviewed in the report.

How to work, in this order:
1. `list_candidates` to see the groups, worst first. Plan by group counts: about one finding per root cause, not per result.
2. Open each important group with the `rule` argument and read the measurements. Ask of every group: is it real, what causes it, what does it do to this board's job? A candidate flag (a ground reference taken from an outline, a zone without fill data) means an artifact of the file, not a fault.
3. Record findings. Use `record_finding` with `rule_group` to cover a whole group in one finding, or with `candidate_ids` to cover the part of a group that shares one cause. Split a group when causes or consequences differ (capacitors far from the radio's supply pins and far from a sensor are two findings). Take the range and worst case from `class_summary` (min and max) or the group `statistics`, not by eye. Count objects, not records: `rule_totals`, `rule_summary`, `net_summary` (`gap_events`, `nets_with_gaps`): "41 gap events on 9 nets", never "41 signals".
4. Resolve what is left. `dismiss_rule_group` dismisses what remains of a rule with one specific reason that names this board ("generated by the unfilled ground zone; the file has no fill data, so no gap can be measured"). Do not dismiss medium or high results with a vague reason: say why they are harmless, or record them. `dismiss_candidate` is for single results.
5. Open review. Use `view_board` with `around` (for example ref:U3, net:ANT_FEED, zone_role:radio, or a candidate id) and a margin, and `annotate` to see candidate marks on the image. Look at: the radio and its supply pins, the antenna, its feed and keep-out, the crystal, power entry and regulation, the USB or other fast interface and its return path, board edges, and the brief's risks. Record what the rules do not express: placement and orientation, test points, parts crowded at the edge, connector placement, fragile routing. Open-review findings are engineering opinion unless a tool measured them; say so, and say what would settle them.

Placement and routing judgement (the server refuses `advance_phase` with `layout_review_incomplete` until it is done):
The layout groups are zones, noise_proximity, track_jitter, track_detour, track_angle, edge_track, connector_access and via_count. You need at least {layout_findings} finding(s) that judge placement or routing. They count when they come from those groups (`record_finding` with `rule_group` or `candidate_ids`: the analyzer's own marks are then drawn on the board for you, and zones get a region mark) or when they are open-review opinions you formed in a close-up region view and anchored with real ids (a part, a track, a net, a zone, a candidate; never invented coordinates, and not a whole-board remark).
What is valued:
- power parts not grouped: regulator, inductor, input and output capacitors scattered instead of placed as one tight loop;
- parts placed awkwardly relative to connectors, or a connector with its cables, ESD parts or filter on the far side of the board;
- a cable connector whose mouth does not face out over a board edge (connector_access): use the facing, the board in front of it, the parts in the plug's path and the nearest edge; say whether a plug can reach it and which edge or turn would fix it, as steps for the engineer;
- shaky tracks (track_jitter), acute corners where a clean bend would do (track_angle), tracks running along the board edge (edge_track), via-heavy routes (via_count);
- long detours when a cleaner route exists (track_detour): add a `sketch` mark for the route you suggest (display only, never a change to the board);
- noisy parts or tracks beside sensitive ones (noise_proximity), and function zones that overlap or interleave (zones).
Say how you know (the candidate's measurement) or that it is engineering opinion. If a layout group truly has nothing worth a finding on this board, give a specific reason of at least {layout_reason_chars} characters for that group, either with `dismiss_rule_group` or with `layout_reasons` on `advance_phase` ({"group": "reason"}); a vague reason does not count.

`via_annular_ring` (vias with too little copper ring around the drill: how many, from `rule_summary`; the fab minimum is for the fab to confirm) and `netclass_width` (tracks off the project's net class width: which class, what width) are not layout judgement but must be reviewed too. So is `pair_skew` (pairs named _DP/_DM, D+/D-, _P/_N: length mismatch in mm and estimated ps).

What good findings look like:
- Rank by consequence on this board. Real shorts and crossing tracks, a radio supply or crystal done badly, a broken return path under a fast signal, and an antenna feed that is exposed, come before neat-and-tidy items. Give the severity you can defend.
- Say what you measured with tool numbers and where. Impedance values are estimates: state the stackup they assume (er, height, plane) and any `reference_plane_missing`.
- Say why it matters here, using the brief's purpose, then what to do as steps for the engineer. Never say you changed anything.
- A board with several hundred results and a radio usually deserves eight to fifteen findings; fewer only when it is clean, and then say what you checked.

Antenna rule: no ground under an antenna and no change inside its keep-out without simulated evidence; the feed line before the antenna starts can be judged on measurements. Note suspected copper or parts near the antenna as suspects to simulate.

Quote only numbers from tools and say nothing was changed. If something cannot be measured, record it as an opinion and say what would settle it. Read the distance before you write "near". Read the zone-fill flags. Do not repeat a request that has an answer. Note suspects only a simulation can settle in a finding. Call `advance_phase` when done; if this review cannot simulate, the server skips ahead to the report.

Rules the server checks (details in the system message):
- Before advice to change the schematic or board, call `read_schematic` kind `state` with refs and quote it. Name every do-not-populate part; do not guess why.
- Name a supply pin by its pin name (`ic_pin_name`). A cause needs its evidence code in the sentence. Severity follows the analyzers' worst hint unless you give `severity_reason`.
- A `netclass_width` result on an RF net near the target is the designer's 50 ohm intent: put it in the impedance finding (it cannot be dismissed). One width plan per feed: not "size for 50 ohm" in one finding and "keep the width" in another.
- Zones group a passive with the active part it shares a net with: a sensor's pull-ups are its own bus, not a noise problem.
