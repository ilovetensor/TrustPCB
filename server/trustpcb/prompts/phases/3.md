# Phase 3: Review

Goal: decide what every analyzer and KiCad result means for this board, then look for what no rule can see. You have {phase3_calls} tool calls and {images} images at most for the whole review. This is the phase where a review is won or lost: spend your calls on judgement, not on browsing.

The server will not let you leave this phase until both of these are true:
- every rule group from `list_candidates` is resolved, which means its results are recorded in findings or dismissed with a reason (a group does not need one finding per result);
- you have taken at least {region_views} close-up region view(s) with `view_board` (an `around` target or a `region`; a view of the whole board does not count).
- the layout has been judged (see "Placement and routing judgement" below): at least {layout_findings} finding(s) from the layout groups or from anchored open-review opinions, or a specific reason for each layout group that has none.
A refused `advance_phase` costs one call. If the calls run out the review moves on and everything unreviewed is listed as not reviewed in the report.

How to work, in this order:
1. `list_candidates` to see the groups, worst first. Use the group counts to plan: you have roughly one finding per root cause, not per result.
2. Open each important group with the `rule` argument and read the measurements. Ask of every group: is it real, what causes it, and what does it do to this board's job? Check the flags in a candidate (for example a ground reference taken from an outline instead of real fill, or a zone without fill data): those mean an artifact of the file, not a fault.
3. Record findings. Use `record_finding` with `rule_group` to cover a whole group in one finding, or with `candidate_ids` to cover the part of a group that shares one cause. Split a group when its results have different causes or different consequences: for example three decoupling capacitors far from the radio's supply pins and two far from a sensor are two findings of different weight. Put the range and the worst case in the text ("between the nearest and the farthest capacitor, worst on C13").
4. Resolve what is left. `dismiss_rule_group` dismisses what remains of a rule with one specific reason that names this board ("generated by the unfilled ground zone; the file has no fill data, so no gap can be measured"). Do not dismiss medium or high results with a vague reason: say why they are harmless, or record them. `dismiss_candidate` is for single results.
5. Open review. Use `view_board` with `around` (for example ref:U3, net:ANT_FEED, zone_role:radio, or a candidate id) and a margin, and `annotate` to see candidate marks on the image. Look at: the radio and its supply pins, the antenna, its feed line and its keep-out, the crystal and its load, power entry and regulation, the USB or other fast interface and its return path, board edges, and anything the brief called a risk. Record what you see that the rules do not express: component placement and orientation, missing or hard-to-reach test points, parts crowded against the edge, connector placement, fragile routing, anything a layout reviewer would flag. Open-review findings are engineering opinion unless a tool measured them; say so, and say what would settle them.
6. `show_on_board` lets the engineer watch what you are looking at.

Placement and routing judgement (the server refuses `advance_phase` with `layout_review_incomplete` until it is done):
Engineers want your judgement of the layout drawn on the board, not only the rule list. The layout groups are zones, noise_proximity, track_jitter, track_detour, track_angle, edge_track and via_count. You need at least {layout_findings} finding(s) that judge placement or routing. They count when they come from those groups (`record_finding` with `rule_group` or `candidate_ids`: the analyzer's own marks are then drawn on the board for you, and zones get a region mark) or when they are open-review opinions you formed in a close-up region view and anchored with real ids (a part, a track, a net, a zone, a candidate; never invented coordinates, and not a whole-board remark).
What is valued:
- power parts not grouped: regulator, inductor, input and output capacitors scattered instead of placed as one tight loop;
- parts placed awkwardly relative to connectors, or a connector with its cables, ESD parts or filter on the far side of the board;
- shaky tracks (track_jitter), acute corners where a clean bend would do (track_angle), tracks running along the board edge (edge_track), via-heavy routes (via_count);
- long detours when a cleaner route exists (track_detour): add a `sketch` mark for the route you suggest. A sketch is display only and is never a change to the board; word it as a suggestion for the engineer to apply in their own tool;
- noisy parts or tracks beside sensitive ones (noise_proximity), and function zones that overlap or interleave (zones).
Say how you know (the measurement the candidate gave) or say it is engineering opinion and what would settle it. If a layout group truly has nothing worth a finding on this board, give a specific reason of at least {layout_reason_chars} characters for that group, either with `dismiss_rule_group` or with `layout_reasons` on `advance_phase` ({"group": "reason"}); a vague reason does not count.

What good findings look like:
- Rank by consequence on this board. Real shorts and crossing tracks, a radio supply or crystal done badly, a broken return path under a fast signal, and an antenna feed that is exposed, come before neat-and-tidy items. Give the severity you can defend.
- Say what you measured with the numbers a tool gave you (distances, lengths, counts, impedance estimates) and where on the board. Impedance values are estimates from the stackup; call them estimates.
- Say why it matters here, using the board's purpose from the brief. Then say what to do, as steps the engineer applies in their own tool. Never say you changed anything.
- Aim for depth: a board with several hundred results and a radio usually deserves eight to fifteen findings. Fewer is fine only when the board is clean, and then say what you checked.

Antenna rule: never recommend ground under an antenna, or any change inside its keep-out, without simulated evidence. The feed line up to where the antenna starts can be judged on measurements. The radiating section cannot. If you suspect copper or a part near the antenna, note it as a suspect to simulate.

Quote only numbers from tools. Do not say anything was changed. If something looks wrong but you cannot measure it, still record it as an opinion, say so, and say what would settle it. Look at one thing at a time, and do not repeat a request that already has an answer.

When you have a short list of suspects that only a simulation can settle (for example "copper near the antenna is detuning it"), note them in a finding or your status text. Call `advance_phase` when done. If this review cannot simulate, the server skips ahead to the report.
