# Phase 5: Confirm

Goal: make sure a claimed improvement is real. Screen runs only compare; a detailed run proves. You have {phase5_calls} tool calls and {confirm_runs} confirm runs in total (a refine round adds a few calls and one confirm run).

Do this:
1. `confirm_result` with `which` set to `base`, then `winner`. The winner is a pair: what-if changes plus matching parts. A matching-only winner reuses the base run, so it costs one run. The server then re-tunes the matching at confirm quality, first with the screen's parts locked and then free, and reports both.
2. If the confirm run disagrees with the screen run, believe the confirm run and say so. The answer carries a `verdict` (outcome, hold, held, meets_target, evidence codes); the server also rewrites the screen-phase conclusions of the investigation to follow it and keeps your screen text as history.
3. If the improvement does not hold, say it plainly: "the screen improvement did not hold at confirm quality". The server records it in the finding; do not hide it or soften it.
4. A confirm result can land between a failure and a win: `improved_short_of_target`. That is the outcome when the best confirm-quality candidate, the free re-tune included, improves the worst in-band S11 by at least 3 dB and still misses the target. It is a partial win, not a failure. The finding must then (a) cite the confirm evidence rows (the confirm base, the best candidate, the confirm comparison), (b) name the best confirm-quality parts as a concrete recommendation for the engineer to apply in their own tool (refs and values, for example "fit C20 with 0.3 pF and replace L1 with a 0.47 pF series capacitor"), (c) give the accepted power before and after (mismatch only, not radiated power or efficiency), and (d) say plainly that the target is not met. `advance_phase` is refused with `partial_win_unreported` until the finding does; the reports and the summary lead with the partial win.
5. Update the finding with `record_finding` (use `update` with its code). Rewrite its prose from the `verdict`, not from your screen-phase notes: quote each number with its evidence code and the word simulated, name the run each number is for, and say whether the improvement held (`held`) and whether the target is met (`meets_target`). The server refuses text that contradicts the verdict. A finding about the antenna quotes confirm numbers only, from confirm evidence with trust ok. Candidate changes are worded as tested in simulation, for the engineer to apply in their own tool. Do not call a dip that stayed fixed when the length changed the antenna's resonance; the first series resonance is the length-related indicator.

## If the confirm run misses the target: the refine round

A confirm run that ends short of the target ({target_db} dB across {band}) is not the end of the work. When the winner's confirm outcome is `held_smaller`, `not_held`, or `held_after_retune` and the target is still not met, `advance_phase` does not take you to the report. The server opens one bounded refine round on the same investigation instead (at most one per Standard review, two per Deep, none on Quick). The phase stays 5; the round is a second attempt, not a new phase. Budget walls, stop rules and honesty checks all still apply.

Before you call `advance_phase`, read the confirm evidence and think about what it says: how far each confirm number is from the target, what the screen overstated, and which change (shortening, matching, both) the confirm numbers support. The brief for the round arrives when it opens. In short:
- up to {refine_screens} screen what-ifs with `run_simulation`, each with a `reason` that cites confirm evidence by its code (for example E-004) and names what you expect it to change;
- `tune_matching` on the what-if that helps most, so geometry and matching are tried together; this was the untried combination in earlier reviews;
- then one `confirm_result` with `which` set to `refine` for the new best candidate (geometry and matching together).
On Standard you cannot decline while solver budget allows; you must try at least one combined candidate. On Deep you may decline with `decline_refine`, a specific reason that cites confirm evidence and says why the base is hopeless. Never skip a round just because you are tired of the investigation.

After the round, update the finding with `record_finding` (use `update`):
- If the new candidate meets the target at confirm quality, state the combined fix exactly as tested in simulation: the geometry change and the matching parts together, quoting confirm numbers only, worded for the engineer to apply in their own tool.
- Otherwise state both attempts honestly, from the refine `verdict`: what the first confirm showed, what the refine candidate showed with its matching parts (locked and free, each with its code) and not the geometry-only run, and that neither met the target. Do not say the refine candidate "did not hold" when its outcome says it held. Do not claim a fix. Say what remains to be tried on a real board. Give no cause for a miss that no run varied.

Then call `advance_phase`.
